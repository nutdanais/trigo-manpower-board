How spreading works

Move earlier: yes

Calibrate a tool before its due date. The next due date becomes the calibration date plus its interval, so the pile-up breaks up for good.

Move later: no

Past the due date a tool is overdue. None of the options pushes a date back.

The cost

A tool loses the weeks it was pulled forward, once. Every option caps this (default 10 weeks). Please check this against your quality procedure.

B

Spread assistant

Set a limit per week and how early a tool may be pulled. The assistant proposes every move, shows before and after on the chart, and you untick what you disagree with.

Build: medium
Dashboard › Tools › Calibration planner › Spread assistant

Rules

Each tool goes to the nearest earlier week with room, so it loses as little of its cycle as possible. In-house tools move first. Nothing moves past its due date.

Try it: lower the limit to 3 or the window to 2 weeks and watch what can no longer be placed. Then untick a few moves and apply.

Good

  • Fixes a whole fleet in one go, and shows the effect first.
  • The dashed bars show where each week was before.
  • Rules are visible, so the result is easy to explain to a lab.

Watch

  • Less control over which tool goes where.
  • A good result depends on the limit being right. It needs a setting for lab capacity.

Recommendation: A first, then B inside it

  1. Ship the card changes and A. This is the smallest set that answers "see in detail" and lets you act.
  2. Add B as a "Spread…" button in A's toolbar. It reuses A's planned date, so it is mostly the move rules and one screen.
  3. Leave C for later. B and C both edit the same planned date, so C can be added if people want to arrange weeks by hand.

What the data needs

  • A planned calibration date on each tool, separate from the due date.
  • A limit per week setting, one number to start, per lab later.
  • A short log of moves: who moved what, from which week.
  • After a calibration: next due = date done + interval.

Questions for you

  • Is there a real limit on calibrations per week, and is it per lab or per site?
  • Is calibrating early allowed, and how early at most?
  • Who calibrates: in-house, outside labs, or both? Should the plan treat them separately?
  • Should the dashboard chart show planned dates once they exist? (The mockups do.)
