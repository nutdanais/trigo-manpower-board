A private what-if sandbox on the Capacity tab

Yes, this is possible, and it needs no new database table. The numbers an engineer has to collect by hand today are already on the running board, so the tab can fill itself. Each person's plan stays in their own browser, so two people planning the same board never touch each other's numbers.

Where we are

Today's tab is a shared sheet, not a sandbox

How it works today

  • One sheet per board, saved in the database. Everyone sees and edits the same numbers live.
  • A row is a host and shift. The people count has to be typed, or filled once with Start from confirmed plan, which only fills empty cells.
  • A what-if by one engineer overwrites what colleagues see. Reset to 0 exists so "the next planner" gets a clean sheet.
  • One engineer rarely knows the crew at every host, so the sheet is often incomplete and the gap is not trusted.

What you asked for, with your answers

  • Opens already filled with the running missions of the board the person picks.
  • Private. Edits are only for that person. Sharing is by Export.
  • One sandbox per board. Switching board shows that board's numbers only.
  • Kept on this device until the person resets it.
  • Rows are hosts with their missions inside.
  • A new request is entered as people × date range, and the day cells stay editable.
  • The shared team plan stays beside the sandbox.
The same in every option

How the sandbox fits together

All three options below share this mechanism. They differ only in how a person works with it on screen.

Where the starting numbers come from

  • Confirmed days: the people actually placed on each mission that day.
  • Forecast days that have a forecast: the people placed in the forecast.
  • Later days: the latest confirmed day repeated, since running missions normally continue.
  • A mission closed on the Missions tab stops on its closing date.
  • Available is calculated as today: roster minus leave, with loans counted on the borrowing board.

Why people cannot collide

  • The sandbox never writes to the database. It is stored in the browser under the person and the board.
  • Because nothing is written, a viewer can use the sandbox too. Today a viewer can only read.
  • It works in the ?demo preview with no change to the fake database.
  • The sandbox shows when its starting point was pulled. If the board changed since, it offers Refresh.
  • The limit: it does not follow the person to another computer or phone. Export covers that for now.
A

A switch on the same grid

The tab gets a switch: Team plan (shared, as today) or My sandbox. The sandbox is the grid people already know, pre-filled, with hosts opening to their missions. A request is a small form that writes numbers into the cells. Changed cells are tinted and show the old number.

Good

  • Smallest change. The grid, copy and paste, phone layout and export are reused.
  • Nothing new to learn for people who use the tab today.

Cost

  • A request becomes plain numbers once entered. It cannot be switched off to compare, only undone by hand.
  • Refresh from board has to choose, cell by cell, between the board's new number and the person's edit.
B

Requests on top of the running board

Recommended

The sandbox keeps two layers apart: the running board as the starting point, and a list of the person's requests on top. Each request can be ticked on or off, and the grid shows the outcome with before and after. Cells can still be edited; an edit is saved as one more change in the list. Try the tick boxes below.

Good

  • Answers the real question, "what happens if we accept this request", by ticking it on and off.
  • Refresh is safe: the starting point is replaced and the requests are laid over it again.
  • The export can list the requests, so a reader sees what was assumed.
  • The same request list could later be saved to an account or published to the team plan.

Cost

  • More to build than A: the request list, and the rule that a cell edit becomes a change in the list.
  • On a phone the list and the grid are two screens.
C

Ask one question, get one answer

For the person who only wants to know whether a request fits. A short form on top of the tab; the answer is one sentence, the days that are short, and the ways to cover them. Open in grid leads to the full sandbox for anything more. It works well on a phone.

My recommendation

Build B, in two steps, and add C later as its front door. Step 1 is the part A and B share: the sandbox switch, the automatic fill from the running board, hosts opening to missions, storage per person and board on the device, and the export label. That step alone removes the number collecting. Step 2 adds the request list with its tick boxes. C can follow as a short form that creates one request in the same sandbox.

What a preview on ?demo will show: all of the above, since nothing touches the database. What it will not prove: the numbers on your real boards, and how large real boards look in the grid.

Before building

Questions for you

  1. Which option? A, B, or B with C added later.
  2. Publish to the team plan? Should a person be able to send their sandbox numbers to the shared Team plan, or does the Team plan stay typed by hand?
  3. Is the Team plan still needed? If every sandbox fills itself from the board, the shared sheet may lose its purpose. It could be kept as is, or retired after a trial.
  4. Changing Available? Should the sandbox also allow "2 new hires from 1 Dec" or "borrow 3 from another board", or is demand enough for a first version?
  5. Which mode opens first? My sandbox or Team plan when someone opens the Capacity tab.
  6. Managers with several boards: is one board at a time enough, or is a view across boards needed later?

What I would revisit as use grows

  • Saving to the account (private rows in the database) if people want their sandbox on phone and computer. Your choice of "this device" avoids a migration for now.
  • Sending a sandbox to a colleague as a file they can open in their own sandbox, if Export proves too static.
  • Skills and qualifications: the gap counts heads only, so 5 spare people may not be the 5 a customer needs.
