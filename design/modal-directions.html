3 · Review & merge forecast, rebuilt around decisions

What is hard to understand today

  1. Two words nobody defines. "Carry-over" and "Forecast" appear everywhere, and the meaning is hidden in one dense paragraph.
  2. Meaning carried by line style. "Solid" and "dashed" outlines tell you which side is which, and four chip styles (same, needs a decision, kept, not used) need a legend.
  3. You decide by clicking a tiny name tag. It is unclear what the click does, and the same person can show up in two rows (where they are now, and where the forecast sends them).
  4. The effect is not in the choice. A chip says "Somchai" with a small arrow. It never says "Somchai stays on 0007" or "Somchai moves to 0011".
  5. Bulk actions in three places: a toolbar, per-forecaster chips, and per-mission buttons.
  6. The ending is a surprise. Reasons for forecasters appear after the list, the Confirm step is a separate screen, and the footer button says "Apply (3 left)" then flashes something.

The new design

The page now asks one question per card: which version do we keep? Each option states its result in plain words. Try it: choose a side on each card. Choose "Don't add" on the new mission to see the locked cards, or choose a carry-over side on a forecaster's card to see the optional reason appear. The footer button tells you what is left.

What changed, in one line each

  • Plain words first: Carry-over is the plan carried from the day before, the forecast is what engineers planned. Said once at the top, dismissible.
  • One decision per card, with the result in the button: "Stay on 0007 Day" or "Move to 0011 Day".
  • No line-style code. State is a word ("Needs a decision", "Selected") plus colour.
  • One bulk bar that acts on what you are looking at.
  • Reasons sit on the card they belong to, only when needed.
  • Summary is the last rail step, in the same dialog, with the alerts that will go out.
