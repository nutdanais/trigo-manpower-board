Samples 2 to 4 · Three ways to handle leave

The leave rows are the biggest block above the missions: five full-width rows, 378 px tall with today's roster (3 leave types in use). Each option below keeps the employee cards and the leave card labels exactly as they are today, and only changes where they sit.

A Leave columns

  • Five zones side by side in one band, each with today's label and its employee cards below.
  • Every zone is a drop target all the time, nothing hides or expands.
  • A zone with many people grows downward, so the band can still get tall.

B Leave strip

  • One slim strip that lists only the people who are on leave, grouped by type, as normal employee cards.
  • "Show all leave types" opens the five drop zones when you need them.
  • Empty types are not visible until opened, so dropping onto one takes an extra step.

C Leave in the side panel

  • The right-hand panel already holds the people who are not on a mission. Leave joins Standby and On-call there.
  • The board area starts with the missions straight away.
  • The panel is only 264 px wide, so the cards stack one per row and the panel grows by about 632 px. On a laptop the last leave types sit below the fold.

My recommendation: A. It more than halves the leave block (from 378 px to 151 px), keeps all five drop targets visible, and needs no new interaction. Column widths follow the number of people, so a busy leave type gets room and an empty one stays small. B gives the most space back on the board but hides the empty leave types until you open them. C moves the problem rather than solving it: the board is clear, but the 264 px panel becomes a long scroll and drag and drop to the lower leave types gets harder. If you prefer C, the panel width (it is already resizable) would need to grow to about 340 px so two cards fit per row.
