What changes, section by section

Numbered in the new order. Improve keeps a section's job and makes it read better; Rework rebuilds it to answer a better question.

1

Board selector and toolbar

Shipped + small
Before
The new board pills, plus the old toolbar strip "All employees 40 · Board One 28 · Board Two 12".
After
Pills only. The toolbar keeps just Export. On a phone, the board-only "Board…" select and "More" button are hidden on Overview.

The pills already carry each headcount, and the phone gets about 120px of space back before the first number.

2

Attention card

Improve
Before
Two boxes: the status bar ("Needs attention today") and the Action queue, both saying 20 people are idle.
After
One card: the headline, today's shifts in plain words, idle names grouped by board (first 6, then "+N more"), and on-call free folded into one calm line.

One fact, said once. Grouping by board answers "where" without reading a caption. The service area stays on each name because there is more than one area.

3

KPI tiles

Improve
Before
A five-colour bar with names only in tooltips. "▲ 7 pts vs the previous 5 days · 14-day trend".
After
A legend with counts under the Deployed bar, gaps between segments, and the trend stated once: "last 5 working days vs the 5 before".

Readable on a phone, where nobody hovers. Idle red and on-call orange are almost impossible to tell apart, so free on-call is grey here and the two colours never touch.

4

By board

Improve
Before
A bar in every cell, "Assigned" (the KPI says "Deployed"), no Leave or On-call free, the formula printed under the title, and half the columns off-screen on a phone.
After
One bar (Utilized), plain numbers elsewhere, Leave and On-call free columns, the formula behind ⓘ, row click scopes the page, and an arrow opens the board. On a phone each board becomes a card.

A table is for exact values, and one word per idea across the page.

5

By engineer

Improve
Before
A coloured missions bar and a crew bar per engineer, a zero row for every free engineer, and rows that went nowhere.
After
Missions as a number with Day/Night tags, crew split into permanent and on-call with labels, free engineers on one line ("Free today: Eng Three"), and a row click opens the board filtered to that engineer. Now first in "In detail", next to Shifts.

With 8 engineers and 3 working, five zero rows hid the three that matter. When one board is picked, "free" means free on that board.

6

Shifts (was Day / night split)

Improve
Before
"8 DAY" here, "Day: 3" in the status bar, and per-board rows reading "7 / 1".
After
Always "crew · missions", and each board row spelled out: "Day 7 · Night 1 crew".

The page stops appearing to contradict itself.

7

Host coverage (was Host coverage risk)

Rework
Before
A count of people who have ever worked each host, with no word on whether they are available, and a note naming a database table.
After
Each thin host shows its people and where they are today. Red means the only backup is unavailable today, amber means thin but covered.

A host is only at risk when the few people who know it are away. The data is already loaded: deployment history plus today's board.

8

By service area

Improve
Before
Headcount per area split permanent / on-call, with each count only in a tooltip and a percentage line under the total.
After
The same permanent / on-call split, as you asked, with both counts on every row ("12 P · 4 OC"), the biggest area first, totals and percentages on the All areas row, and it follows the board selector.

You can read how many people of each contract type each area has without hovering.

9

History

Rework
Before
Six lines on one axis (Idle around 28 next to Missions around 0.5), two of them crossed out in the legend by default, and a separate "% Utilization" mode.
After
Four average tiles (Utilization, Deployed, Idle, Missions). Pick one to see it day by day on its own axis, with an average line and a hover readout.

One measure per chart, so no line is squashed flat by another's scale. The tiles answer most visits without a chart at all.

10

Engineer workload

Rework
Before
One line per engineer, which tangles past four engineers, a separate five-column table, and crew shown as a single number.
After
One block, with Crew split into permanent and on-call for every engineer, so a manager can see each engineer's team mix. Four designs to compare are in the mockup; the table below compares them.

The data is already there: the History query returns each engineer's permanent and on-call crew per day, so no extra loading is needed.

Engineer workload · pick one

Four ways to show engineer workload

All four use the same numbers and follow the board selector and History's range. Try each one in the mockup above with the "Design option" switch, in both Missions and Crew.

DesignBest forCrew split per engineer10+ engineers90-day rangePhone
Ranked bars + trend RecommendedComparing engineers: who carries the most, and with what mix of peopleClear: a split bar and the numbers on every rowGood: one row eachGood: the trend line compressesGood
Small multiplesEach engineer's day-by-day pattern and mix togetherClear: split on every day's columnWeak: one card each, the page gets longOK: columns get thinOK
Day gridSpotting gaps, streaks and busy days across a long rangeWeak: a thin strip under each numberGoodBest: most compactWeak: scrolls sideways
Stacked columnsThe team's total per day and who carried itHidden: only in the tooltipWeak: too many coloursOKOK

My pick: Ranked bars + trend. It answers your question directly (each engineer's crew and its permanent / on-call mix) in the least space, and it still scales to many engineers and a phone. If day-by-day gaps matter more to you, Small multiples is the runner-up.
