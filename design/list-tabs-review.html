Manpower, Host and Missions: one way to show a list, a record and a form

I went through the three tabs in the demo: each table, the path from a row to its record, the detail panel and the edit form. The three tabs share a toolbar and summary strip that already work well. Below that line each one has grown its own answer to the same questions, and this page proposes how to bring them back together. Nothing in the app has changed yet. The build brief is design/handover-list-tabs.md.

What already worksThe toolbar, the stat tiles with the proportion bar, and the By area chips are the same on all three tabs. Keep them.
The main problemStatus, board, service area and alerts are drawn and placed differently on each tab, and a row leads to a different kind of panel on each.
What was chosenTwo-line rows, a read-first panel with Edit as a second step, and alerts in the status cell: the status chip on the first line, the alert under it.

Part 2 · Against common practice

Where the tabs stand against what established products do

These are the conventions that the large design systems (IBM Carbon, Google Material, Atlassian, Salesforce Lightning) and the products people use daily for people, sites and jobs (Workday, Rippling, Jira, Linear, Airtable, ServiceNow) have converged on. I am describing the patterns from knowledge of those systems, not quoting them.

The first column names the record and stays put

Carbon, Material, Airtable, JiraPartlyIdentity is first everywhere, but bold on two tabs and plain on Manpower, and it scrolls away.

One status per row, one shape, fixed colour meanings

Atlassian lozenge, Carbon status indicatorNoA switch on two tabs, up to three stacked chips on the third.

Status is told by colour plus a shape or word, never colour alone

Carbon, WCAG 1.4.1YesChips carry words. The switch does not say "Active".

Text aligns left, numbers align right, headers stay on one line

Material, Carbon, NN/gPartlyNumbers are right-aligned. Seven headers wrap.

A table cell holds a fact, a list of things belongs in the record

NN/g on data tables, Salesforce related listsNoHost shows up to eight people per cell. Missions stacks three chips.

Row actions: one visible, the rest behind ⋯ at the row's end. Select rows for bulk actions

Carbon overflow menu and batch actions, LinearPartlyMissions does this. Host and Manpower each do something else.

A row opens a read-first side panel. Edit is a clear second step

Linear and Notion peek, Workday and Rippling profilesPartlyManpower and Missions do. Host goes straight to a form.

The record starts with a highlights header: name, status, two or three actions, the rest behind More

Salesforce highlights panel, ServiceNowNoThe mission panel shows six buttons in a row. Status is one tile among twelve.

Fields are label and value pairs. Cards are kept for the few key numbers

Salesforce, Carbon structured listNoEvery field is a card.

Save and Cancel are pinned at the bottom right. Destructive actions sit apart

Material, Carbon side panelPartlyHost and Mission do. Employee's Save is in the middle of the page.

People choose their columns, and the choice is remembered

Jira, Airtable, Linear display optionsPartlyMissions only.

Alerts are counted in one place and each count filters the list

ServiceNow, Jira filters, GmailPartlyThe No crew tile does this. The other alerts do not.

And four layout rules

  • One column order on every tab: who or what it is, status, where (board and service area), the facts specific to the tab, dates, numbers, notes, then ⋯. Whatever tab you are on, status is the second thing you read.
  • One empty value: a grey "—" in tables and panels. A missing value the user should fill is an action ("+ Add"), shown on hover only.
  • One way to act on a row: click opens the record, a checkbox selects for bulk actions, ⋯ at the row's end holds the rest. All three tabs get checkboxes and a Columns button.
  • Shorter facts: "7y 8m" in place of "7 years 8 months 26 days", "Day 08:00–17:00" as one cell, headers on one line.

Why B, A, A

  • Two-line rows are the only option where all three tables fit a 1,366px laptop without sideways scrolling, and where the first three columns are identical on every tab. That is the alignment you asked for.
  • Read, then Edit is what Manpower and Missions already do, so it is the smallest change in habit, and it protects master data from stray clicks. Edit in place is faster but needs every field to save on its own, which the mission form's day-by-day rules make risky.
  • Alerts in the status cell keep the problem next to the record it belongs to, and the tile gives the count and the filter. A strip repeats what the rows already say; a separate column is empty on most rows.
